What to do about it

Four moves grounded directly in the patterns above, ordered by how directly the data supports them.

1

Close the Beauty revenue gap with a bundle push

Beauty trails Clothing and Electronics by roughly 8–10% in total revenue despite a similar transaction count. A bundle discount or loyalty multiplier on Beauty items is the most direct lever to close that gap without acquiring new customers.

2

Target the specific age × category cells, not whole segments

The segment heatmap above shows high average order value concentrated in a few age-group/category intersections. Premium offers — extended warranties, styling add-ons — will earn a better return aimed at those specific cells than at an entire age group or category.

3

Introduce quantity-based bundle pricing

Basket sizes distribute evenly across 1 to 4 units (approx. 25% each) rather than clustering at 1 item. A "buy 3+, save 10%" structure would nudge 1–2 unit baskets upward and lift total transaction value directly.

4

Deprioritize age as a segmentation axis

Age shows almost no correlation with spend or quantity in the correlation matrix. CRM and email targeting built primarily around age brackets is unlikely to outperform targeting based on actual purchase history.
