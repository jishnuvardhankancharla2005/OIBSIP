Where did every missing value actually go?

Not all missing data was handled the same way. Because Total = Quantity × Price holds exactly in this business, most gaps were mathematically recovered rather than guessed. This chart shows exactly how each column's missing values were resolved.

Observation: Total Spent recovered 100% mathematically — every missing total was rebuilt from quantity × price, with zero guessing. Item is the only column where a real "Unknown" label was necessary, because two pairs of items (Cake/Juice and Sandwich/Smoothie) happen to share the same price, so roughly half its gaps couldn't be resolved with certainty. Payment Method and Location were unrecoverable from any other column — they're explicitly labeled "Unknown" rather than silently guessed at with the majority class.
02

Outlier detection — investigate before you delete

The IQR method flags every $25 transaction as a statistical outlier. But is that actually bad data?

Distribution of Total Spent, with IQR bounds

The shaded band beyond the dashed line is what IQR flags as "outlier" territory
Decision — retain, don't remove: the 269 transactions above the IQR upper bound of $24 are all exactly $25 — the maximum possible legitimate order (5 units × the $5 Salad, or similar). Quantity (1–5) and Price ($1–$5) both stay within their natural bounds with zero exceptions. These are valid high-value orders, not corrupted data, so they are kept exactly as recorded.

06

Key cleaning decisions

The five calls that shaped this cleanup, and why each one was made.

1

Unify three "missing" representations into one

True blanks, "ERROR", and "UNKNOWN" were all treated as the same missing marker before any analysis began — otherwise the real scope of missingness (6,747 values, not 460) would have gone undetected.

2

Recover mathematically before imputing statistically

Because Total = Quantity × Price is exact and Item → Price is a fixed lookup, most gaps were rebuilt with certainty from other columns in the same row, rather than guessed at with a population average.

3

Prefer an honest "Unknown" over a confident guess

Payment Method and Location were ~32–40% missing with no recoverable signal. Mode-imputing them would have manufactured false confidence; labeling them "Unknown" keeps that uncertainty visible to anyone using the data downstream.

4

Don't forward-fill a date you can't infer

Transaction IDs aren't sequential by date, so a missing date's true value can't be inferred from its neighbours. Those 460 rows keep every other field and are simply excluded from date-based analysis.

5

Investigate outliers before removing them

IQR flagged every $25 order as statistically unusual. Checking the underlying quantity and price showed these were valid maximum-value transactions, not errors — so nothing was capped or deleted.
